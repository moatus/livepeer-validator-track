**The weak quality-score result does not rule out output attribution.** A quality score compresses the image toward human preference; a source classifier deliberately retains differences between equally good outputs. The most useful new lead is PromptLA, which actually includes SD1.5 versus SD1.4. Its reported false alarms and hundreds of images per decision also make it a warning against adopting its AUC headline. Prioritize a small learned residual/spectral comparison on saved images before another judge. Treat every positive as statistical source consistency, never proof that a fresh private job executed particular weights.

## Four candidates

### 1. Passive learned local fingerprints, with spectral/residual controls — highest priority

[Livne et al., Scalable Black-Box Model Attribution, v2, 20 September 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15652v2) learns a small multi-patch CNN directly from pixels: 98.9% on 25-class DRAGON, 95.0% on 27-class OpenFake. Open-set AU-OSCR is 0.875 ± 0.037 across five known/unseen splits; **unseen fine-tunes can confidently map to known siblings**. Generation settings are not controlled sufficiently to separate weights from scheduler/guidance/precision. Adaptive adversarial robustness is explicitly untested. High-frequency transplantation can move predictions toward the donor: useful causal evidence for low-level signal, also a forgery warning. The [project](https://asaf-livne.github.io/RPA/) was linked by the paper but inaccessible to this browser; downloadable code/checkpoints were not independently confirmed.

[Xu et al., 2024 v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.19653v2) demonstrates attribution across 12 generators and inference variations, including seeds; high-frequency perturbation only slightly reduces its accuracy and style representations can outperform RGB. Therefore a frequency-only negative would not settle attribution either. [Official demo/code](https://github.com/k8xu/ImageAttribution) exposes a 118 MB EfficientFormer checkpoint link, a demo and 10 test images/class; its README reports 90.03%. This is a deployment demo, not evidence of a complete reproducible training pipeline or of SD1.4/1.5 separation. The [WACV2025 paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2025/papers/Xu_Detecting_Origin_Attribution_for_Text-to-Image_Diffusion_Models_WACV_2025_paper.pdf) uses a different final title; do not conflate versions.

**Access and claim:** labeled reference images, delivered output pixels, and training a modest classifier; no target weights or provider cooperation. A single-image score is possible after many-image enrollment. Existing local SD1.x data are suitable only for exploratory screening: 215 images and 24 families are small for learning a forensic network. A frozen small backbone or regularized residual/spectral classifier is more realistic than scratch training. Metadata, resolution, encoding, safety filtering and scheduler shortcuts must be excluded. A model can pass from a genuine replay; no freshness, prompt binding or provider-contact guarantee follows. For private jobs, a client/confidential checker must see pixels, shifting trust.

### 2. Actively chosen prompts and feature distributions — directly relevant close-pair evidence, costly

[PromptLA, v1, 20 December 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.16257v1) uses Inception-v3 feature distributions, approximate Gaussian KL differences and adaptive prompt selection. It explicitly tests SD1.5 against SD1.4 rollback, DreamBooth and parameter perturbations. Table 4 reports rollback AUC 0.928/0.953; corresponding same-model FPRs are **0.100/0.250**, not deployment-ready calibration. Table 5 reports average rollback costs **846/859 images per detection**. Twenty repeats and JPEG-85/crop tests do not establish unseen-version or adaptive-router robustness. Selection and verification are intertwined; a fresh held-out-seed confirmation is essential. No project code link was established in the inspected paper. Its evidence supports investigating distributions and informative prompts, not a cheap per-job identity check.

[CSF, CVPR2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16363), [primary paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/papers/Lee_CSF_Black-box_Fingerprinting_via_Compositional_Semantics_for_Text-to-Image_Models_CVPR_2026_paper.pdf), instead queries underspecified compositions and compares CLIP semantic-category distributions from 30 images/prompt. Its explicit objective attributes fine-tunes **to their base model**: fine-tuning invariance is success here, whereas it may be failure for an exact-checkpoint claim. These are category distributions, not quality grades or target likelihoods. The primary PDF's search-accessible excerpt establishes the method/threat; full PDF retrieval failed (403), so no headline accuracy, interval coverage or code-release claim is adopted.

**Access and claim:** trusted reference enrollment and repeated suspect generation, no target internals. PromptLA can reject a reference without naming the replacement; CSF chiefly answers lineage. Both need honest same-route/configuration controls and unknowns. One 3090 can run SD1.x sequentially, but reproducing full PromptLA's query budget is a separate substantial study. Distinctive repeated/underspecified probes invite routing recognition. Secret prompts lower recognition opportunities but are not evidence that ordinary traffic uses the same route.

### 3. Collapsed-generation consistency — promising low verification query count, poor exact-version fit

[Huang et al., v1, 12 August 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11732v1) selects natural prompts with unusually consistent cross-seed outputs; default verification uses four prompts × four outputs. API-only tests cover SD1.4 and SD2.1; candidates come from public training-extraction prompt sets. Modern SD3/PixArt evidence uses optimized **continuous embeddings**, not an ordinary text-only API; discretization attempts fail its threshold. Fine-tune robustness is a lineage/ownership benefit, not exact-version discrimination. Its stealth test measures prompt perplexity, not an adaptive gateway's routing classifier. Public-pool matching, repetition and account/session signals remain available to a router. No project code link was established.

**Access and claim:** natural-prompt mining needs extensive reference queries or source-training information; small online verification excludes that enrollment expense. No weights needed for the API-only arm, but embedding optimization needs pipeline access. This is a many-call consistency test, not output-by-output attribution. Replay of low-diversity genuine outputs is an obvious freshness counterexample; logging whether gateway recognition changes ordinary routing is required. A local test is possible with existing SD1.x weights, but SD1.4/1.5 discrimination is unestablished. Do not infer it from SD1.4/2.1 success.

### 4. Reconstruction/final-layer inversion — local diagnostic; not a general closed-provider solution

[FLIPAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.06210), [official code](https://github.com/MikeLasz/flipad), needs the exact candidate final-layer weights and learns an anomaly detector on recovered features. Its Stable Diffusion study targets v2.1 against v1.1/v1.4/v2; it does not establish v1.4/1.5 discrimination. It uses 2,000 training, 100 validation and 200 test samples and a 5% target FNR for that experiment. JPEG/noise can reduce performance even with matching perturbations during training. Thus local open-weight testing is feasible, but its published sample requirements exceed our saved corpus and its weight requirement excludes independently checking an inaccessible proprietary checkpoint.

[DIRE, ICCV2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.09295v1), [code](https://github.com/ZhendongWang6/DIRE), compares an image with reconstruction through a reference diffusion model. Its principal claim is **real versus diffusion-generated**, including unseen generators, not exact source identity. Reusing its scalar reconstruction error as a checkpoint verifier would require new evidence. Output likelihood under a known open model or latent reconstruction similarly tests compatibility with that model, not unique provenance: overlapping model distributions and editable pixels matter. Closed image APIs generally exposing only pixels do not supply the denoising trajectory, weights or exact likelihood this would need.

## Strongest counterevidence and adaptive-gateway boundary

[Smudged Fingerprints, v2, 21 January 2026; SaTML2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11771v2), [code](https://github.com/kaikaiyao/SmudgedFingerprints), tests 14 attribution methods, 12 image generators and five attacks across RGB/frequency/learned features. Removal often exceeds 80% success white-box and 50% black-box; forgery is harder but possible and model-dependent. None combines clean accuracy and robustness across all evaluated threats. These are serious pixel-controlling intermediary attacks, not direct measured bypass rates for the later Livne system. Test removal **and targeted false attribution**; merely rejecting attacked samples as unknown helps avoid a false identity claim but still denies verification. Quality-preserving edits and permitted transformations need an explicit contract.

The eight studies above concern images, except no LLM method is being transferred here. KBF, IRIS and LLMmap from the prior dossier offer adjacent text-distribution/query-selection ideas; token likelihood, knowledge boundaries and random-string generation are not image fingerprints. Internal activation checks require runtime access and do not become closed-API checks because a gateway cooperates.

**My inference:** hidden random challenges help only to the degree that audit and ordinary jobs are indistinguishable **to the gateway at routing time**. Natural prose/perplexity is insufficient: the operator sees prompts, repeated patterns, timing, accounts, sizes and traffic context. A router that recognizes audits can route them genuinely while substituting elsewhere; none of the image studies establishes a complete defense against that reseller. Secret randomized seeds do not help if the operator still recognizes the challenge class. Sampling ordinary delivered jobs after commitment is harder to selectively route, but passive attribution must then work on their natural prompt distribution and permitted edits. Even perfect output classification cannot reject a genuine cached replay or prove provider contact/fresh execution. Authentication and request/output binding remain separate evidence.

Confidence must be calibrated on independent same-model controls under honest configuration/drift, with separate unknown generators and particularly unknown sibling checkpoints. Softmax, likelihood ratios, p-values, AUROC and Bayesian lineage intervals are different objects; none is automatically a probability that this customer's job ran model X. Aggregate intervals must respect prompt/session clustering and the adaptive selection procedure.

## At most two bounded next tests — proposals only

1. **Saved-image forensic screen before new generation.** Reuse all 215 saved usable images and frozen 12/6/6 family splits; primary SD1.4 versus SD1.5, SDXL withheld as an unknown stressor. Predeclare at most two representations: fixed noise-residual/log-spectrum features and one small frozen learned image representation with a regularized head. Reuse existing cached weights only; no new judge. Cap one 3090 at two GPU-hours and no generated images. Thresholds: proceed only if held-out close-pair balanced accuracy ≥75%, family-cluster 95% lower bound >50%, and calibrated reference rejection gives ≤10% honest-image false alarms; report coverage and all confidence intervals. Check JPEG85 and normalized encode/resize versions without refitting; a >15 percentage-point loss or a failure of either primary threshold stops this lead for this dataset. Unknown SDXL rejection ≥80% is a screening target, explicitly confounded by architecture/resolution and insufficient for sibling rejection. These thresholds are investigator choices, not literature-established standards. Existing test labels have already been inspected: success authorizes only fresh confirmation, never a confirmatory claim.

2. **Only if screen succeeds: a frozen-distribution, adaptive-router falsification test.** Separately authorize ≤1,200 SD1.x images, sequential local generation, ≤8 GPU-hours on the existing 3090, no hosted calls; no more than 12 enrollment prompts, six calibration families and six untouched test families. Reserve repeated-seed samples independently for selection versus verification; compare passive fingerprints with one fixed Inception-distribution statistic/active-prompt policy, without claiming a full PromptLA reproduction. Mock honest configuration drift, full substitution, 30% random substitution, probe-aware honest routing with ≥90% ordinary-traffic substitution, and replay. Require ≤5% false alarms across ≥100 separately sampled honest audit episodes and ≥80% power for full/30% substitution; repeated episodes reusing images are conditional harness estimates, not 100 independent generation studies. **Reject deployment relevance if probe-aware routing passes ≥90% of audits while ordinary substitution stays ≥90%, or replay passes:** record the resulting evidence boundary and stop, without escalating prompt secrecy as an assumed solution. No extra generation beyond the cap to rescue a failed threshold; if sample precision is insufficient, report inconclusive.
