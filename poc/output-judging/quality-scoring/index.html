MODEL VERIFICATION RESEARCH · Methods, evidence and limitations

What could we measure about a model's outputs?

These tools provide candidate features for a behavioral fingerprint. Their published quality or preference results do not establish generator-identification accuracy. The useful research question is whether their scores add stable information on unseen prompts.

The different forms of data

Single scores · multi-axis scores · checklist outcomes · pairwise preferences · raw model scores/logits · embeddings · patterns of disagreement. Several may be correlated. More measurements are useful only if they add information beyond simpler features and ordinary quality differences.

What the source review corrected

Vouch's nemotron-omni-vision alias maps to nvidia/nemotron-3-nano-omni/vision in its committed fixture and the saved current capability metadata. Grok's README-only scan did not resolve this mapping. Exact weights and serving revision remain unpinned.

The scan found no direct, controlled generator-identification demonstration using this particular collection of scores. That is a bounded literature gap, not evidence that the idea cannot work. Its claim that these profiles are easier to spoof than KBF is a hypothesis, not a comparative result established here.

Vouch's checklists are shared among contenders within a shootout but vary across briefs. Comparisons need matched prompts and consistent skill definitions. Separate judge calls are not automatically independent evidence.

Newly surfaced reward-hacking and rubric-bias papers are cautions and research leads. We did not rerun them or infer their attack rates apply to Vouch. Code licenses and weight licenses can differ; detailed distinctions are in the catalog.

Evidence and scope

We inspected source and documentation, including Vouch commit f400251. No scoring or generation experiment was run. No existing fingerprinting result establishes that these score profiles identify a closed model.
