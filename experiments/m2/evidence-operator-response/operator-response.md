## Fixed merits and selected procedure

Both paths use SC-10's same K1 terms, same output bytes, same job-bound independent observation and same public complaint P0. The allegation is K, not purpose. The job does not breach K1. Nothing in the underlying merits or operator standing changes. Resources/access differ explicitly: SC-25 has a retained indexed bundle, authorized staff able to redact/retrieve it and a working route to notice/reasons/review receipt. SC-26 has the same underlying records but cannot complete authorized retrieval/redaction on that route before claim; the assumed receipt is n+8. This is a hypothetical resource/channel constraint, not an observation that new operators are treated badly or that established operators deserve favorable treatment. Notice itself may be unavailable in practice; this tabletop uses received notice for both to isolate response access.

## Individual ceilings and burdens

`tolerance-assessment.csv` reports all eight IDs separately for each response path/standard/reading. Evidence sufficiency is distinct from actual scorer behavior. The timely path supplies a supported merits correction within the declared one exchange and selective disclosure ceiling; claim-time protection is conditional on the current median acting on it. The late path misses T-T for correction; a's accepted evidence limitation cannot be described as effective detection, and b's possible cut on this legitimate job fails strict false-cut protection. No branch may treat notice failure or silence as evidence of K. No knowing-violation evidence is introduced by either path.

Routine review is P0/source inspection. Contested review adds one focused question, one bundle and same-byte bounded check, with one possible evidence correction inside that exchange. These fit T-ER/T-EC structurally; preparation time, actual review cost and feasibility are unmeasured. SC-25 must retrieve terms, establish custody, obtain output-release authorization, redact metadata, confirm delivery to reviewers and wait for scoring. SC-26 faces the same tasks with fewer retrieval/authorization resources and a longer unresolved-income interval. This is a conditional resource burden, not measured disproportion or incidence. Disclosure exposes job linkage/timing and operator/customer metadata even for non-sensitive bytes. If the indispensable output is private, T-DP forbids assuming it can safely be published; hash-only disclosure would be insufficient for dimensions.

Both operators bear defense effort and income uncertainty even if the final judgment is correct. A corrected median does not erase preparation/disclosure cost. Delegators may face uncertain whole-round rewards and the proposed stake lock; neither principal slashing nor immediate exit follows. Investigators bear collection/authentication/checking; published shared checks may reduce duplicate work if usable, without guaranteeing access, correction or scorer incentives. The significance of this defense burden remains for M2.3.
