# EX-04: tolerances for the self-funded usage comparison

## Alleged violation under each standard

**Narrow standard (I-20).** The allegation must name conduct specified in advance as deceptive or reward-manipulating, the representation or act at issue, and the inflation eligibility advantage sought. Transparent self-payment for real jobs and responsiveness to rewards do not supply that conduct. Unmodified SC-07 specifies accurate payment, execution and correctness records, with no pretence; it contains no specified narrow conduct.

For the narrow comparison only, use an explicitly labelled **SC-07 purpose-misrepresentation variant**: the operator knowingly represents jobs funded only to increase its inflation reward as useful release testing, although no productive or release-testing use accounts for those jobs. The claimed advantage is retaining full inflation eligibility under the assumed narrow criterion while the real payments increase fee input. Payment, execution and correctness remain accurate. This added representation, its falsity and the operator's knowledge must each be examined; they are not inferred from the unmodified case. This is a hypothetical allegation, not an observation or an adopted eligibility rule. SC-23 supplies the legitimate testing comparison.

**Broad standard (I-21).** The allegation against SC-07 is that the organization funds jobs at its commonly controlled node to obtain the resulting inflation reward, with no productive or release-testing use accounting for the jobs. Circular funding means the payer's funds support fees attributed to a commonly controlled node, which can receive the associated reward. It does not mean the entire payment is recovered. Funding, reward purpose and absence of productive/testing use must all be addressed. Common control, a positive return or an unused output alone is insufficient. A release test that also adds fees is the mixed-purpose boundary: reward purpose must be the reason for the job, rather than a side effect of useful testing.

Both standards are possible readings of self-dealing, not settled paper rules (SRC-LP20 L47, L86; VD-09). Whether the reward objective requires the distinction remains I-01/VD-19 and GT-03's separate question.

## Research choices and observation boundary

These are qualitative research tolerances fixed before evidence packets, claim matrices or separation verdicts are written. They are neither empirical error rates nor approved scoring policy. The comparison concerns SC-23, then SC-07 and its labelled narrow variant, under competent review. It ends at the paper-proposed first claim in round n+7 (SRC-LP20 L109, L224). Later evidence cannot establish protection at that first claim.

I-22 carries two possible adverse-judgment readings; the paper specifies neither proof standard nor a disclosure duty:

- **(a) Evidence required:** a cut needs evidence of the alleged violation. Unexplained jobs leave the relevant claim unknown.
- **(b) Explanation burden:** failure to explain the jobs within the delay may warrant a cut, placing the response burden on the operator. This consequence must be examined even where it fails the tolerances below; it must not be silently replaced with reading (a).

A **false cut** reduces the claim-time reward of activity that does not violate the selected standard. A **missed violation** leaves a violating case without an adverse reward consequence by n+7. An unknown claim is not proof of either innocence or violation. Separate these errors from uncertainty about whether the underlying records are accurate. No false-cut or missed-violation frequency can be estimated from this pair.

## Error tolerances by standard and reading

| Standard and I-22 reading | Acceptable false-cut tolerance | Acceptable missed-violation tolerance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Narrow, (a) | Strict: no cut on legitimate SC-23 testing or unmodified SC-07 merely for self-payment, rewards or missing explanation. For the variant, an adverse finding requires support for the specified misrepresentation, falsity, knowledge and eligibility purpose. | An unresolved alleged variant may remain unknown when evidence is unavailable within the stated burden. This is an accepted limitation of the inquiry, not evidence of an effective defense. Ignoring evidence that establishes the specified violation is unacceptable. |
| Narrow, (b) | Strict at n+7: an explanation-based cut on nonviolating activity is unacceptable for a claim of adequate protection. Earlier adverse exposure may be examined, but correction before claim must be supported, not assumed. Failure to furnish a response is not proof of the specified misrepresentation. | An established narrow violation receiving full reward is unacceptable. An unexplained case must retain its evidential uncertainty even if this reading permits a cut; penalizing it does not demonstrate that a violation was detected. |
| Broad, (a) | Strict: no cut solely for common control, reward responsiveness, unused output or absent public proof of use. Useful testing, including mixed-purpose testing, must not be classified as reward-only activity without support for the broad allegation. | Unresolved funding or purpose may remain unknown within the disclosure limit. Report the resulting exposure as a limitation. An established broad violation left without an adverse consequence is unacceptable. |
| Broad, (b) | Strict at n+7: a cut on legitimate testing, including testing whose operator cannot safely disclose its explanation, exceeds tolerance. Examine that exposure explicitly. A bounded explanation supplied in time must not be rejected merely because testing also earns rewards. | An established broad violation receiving full reward is unacceptable. A cut for non-explanation is a procedural consequence under this reading, not proof of reward-only purpose or absence of use. |

The strict false-cut tolerance reflects the potential reach of the median to the node's whole eligible round reward, including rewards associated with other fees (I-19; SRC-LP20 L86, L109). It is a chosen assessment criterion, not a claim that zero error is achievable. Reading (b) can fail it; tolerances do not establish the result in advance. Neither a fractional score nor later recovery is assumed to make an unsupported cut acceptable.

## Reviewer effort per case

Use the same ordinal effort limit for both standards and both readings:

- **Routine, acceptable:** one review of the supplied public records, their sources and arrival times, keeping payment, execution, correctness, independent demand, economic value and dishonesty distinct.
- **Bounded contested review, acceptable:** the routine review plus one focused question and one operator response, inspecting one disclosed source and checking its links to the jobs and stated purpose. The additional source is the release test plan together with the change that followed it, treated as one source bundle. The complete review must be feasible by n+7; availability and timing cannot be presumed.
- **Extended investigation, outside tolerance:** repeated demands, unrelated-job audits, third-party interviews, forensic identity tracing or rebuilding internal systems to establish purpose.

Under reading (a), the additional response is voluntary. Under reading (b), the same bounded response is the maximum acceptable burden for this inquiry; an unanswered question still carries the reading's possible cut. No minutes, monetary costs or demonstrated feasibility are assigned. Shared investigation is the paper's intended support (SRC-LP20 L88), not a measured saving or an assumed evidence feed.

## Operator disclosure and social burden

The acceptable disclosure ceiling is a selectively disclosed, redacted excerpt of the stated source bundle: relevant job references and timing, the testing objective, and the resulting change. The operator or engineers controlling that material supply it. Revealing limited operational metadata and preparing that response are acceptable research burdens; unrestricted access to source code, customer payloads, credentials, business accounts, unrelated job histories or a full release roadmap is not. If necessary redaction removes the support a judgment needs, retain that limitation rather than assuming the excerpt is sufficient. No confidential channel, authenticated archive or enforceable access right is supplied by the paper.

For reading (a), declining disclosure leaves the allegation unresolved unless other obtainable evidence supports it. For reading (b), declining or being unable to disclose can expose the operator to a cut even for legitimate work. Evaluate that exposure against the strict false-cut tolerance; privacy protection is not evidence of dishonest purpose. A successful eventual explanation does not erase its preparation or disclosure cost.

Validators or shared investigators can raise the question; the operator controls the internal explanation. The paper gives active validators individual scores and a median, with mutual accountability and standing to push back (SRC-LP20 L83–88), not a mandatory hearing or guaranteed appeal. While unresolved, reading (a) supplies no evidence-based basis for a cut; reading (b) may permit one. Neither automatically changes other nodes' rewards. Record these dependencies without assuming public accusations, repeated disputes, preferential treatment or costless correction.

## Limits

Synthetic packets test whether a judgment follows from stated assumptions and records. They do not measure accuracy, reviewer effort, practical access, prevalence or operator response cost. Source authenticity, adequate response time and the ability to provide the bounded disclosure remain conditions to check. Missing evidence in this pair cannot establish universal impossibility. Validator incentives and the incidence and cost of contested decisions belong to later inquiries. This document contains no evidence outcomes or separation verdicts.
