## 1. Question and precedent

How much additional retained value can a largest-, middle- or lower-stake operator obtain by deliberately funding real jobs, where would its one-round profit-maximizing spending stop, and which honest participants lose rewards across materially different network conditions? The conditions span today's measured fee scale to a fee-dominant network. Short fixed-budget timelines additionally test spending pace and collection, not a multi-round optimal strategy.

The precedent is EX-06's all-pool accounting and EX-07's separation of economic outcomes from obtainable adverse evidence. EX-03 supplies historical threshold methods; EX-05 supplies the October source and schedule bridge. Extend that accounting with explicit network fixtures, bounded spending optimization and short cohort timelines. A second reward engine or a network simulator is unnecessary. The new scenario adapter is necessary because EX-06's loader and A/B comparisons are pinned to one configuration; its generic accounting function is reusable only through a checked interface.

The owner requested this design before implementation. M2.1 materializes and validates it; it does not choose a new study design. Preserve EX-03 through EX-07, their frozen configurations, historical sources, IDs and original results. Recalculate the scope of conclusions after the additional tests rather than retroactively changing what earlier experiments tested.

## 4. Network and actor definitions

### 4.1 Stable identities and three structures

Order the 100 source pools by completed-round stake descending, breaking ties by address. Keep those source keys stable; display anonymous modeled roles, not allegations about address owners. Synthetic ranks follow that stable ordering and their declared synthetic stake vector. Strategic actors are rank 1 (largest), rank 50 (middle) and rank 90 (lower), selected before outcomes. Report actual shares; rank is not a fixed ownership percentage.

| Structure | Stake vector | Initial outside-fee weights before actor override | Question isolated |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R — measured distribution | Same-round measured stake shares | Same-round measured fee shares | Does the result survive the network-grounded reference distribution? |
| D — distributed mismatch | At rank `k`, stake share `(101-k)/5050` | Fee weight `k/5050` | How does broad misalignment of stake and paid business expose different participants? |
| C — concentrated mismatch | Rank 1: `.20`; ranks 2–10: `.04` each; ranks 11–100: `.44(101-k)/4095` | The same fee weights as D | What changes when stake concentration changes with the pre-override fee weights held fixed? |

All vectors are positive in D/C and sum to one. R retains zero-fee pools. D/C are stress fixtures, not measured populations or predictions. Record normalization with Decimal residual handling at one deterministically selected non-actor pool. Never drop pools to create a desired result.

### 4.2 Outside business and economic market scale

For each actor with stake share `s`, set its initial outside-fee share to `a=h s`, with `h=.25` (substantial reward headroom) or `.90` (near its stake limit). Preserve non-actor relative fee weights by multiplying their weights by `(1-a)/(1-original_actor_weight)`. Record the complete changed vector and differences from the parent. For each fixed actor/start/market, D versus C changes only the declared stake vector and the mechanically required fee reallocation; report those accompanying changes, not a pure causal stake effect.

Let `V0=.94 E0 P0`, where `P0` is EX-06's declared token valuation, and let `R=.94 E P/F` be the reward/fee ratio. Use three core outside-fee totals, ordered as a bootstrapping path from today's condition toward a fee-dominant network: **measured**, `F` equal to the round-4364 replayed fees (`R` approximately 57.6; report its exact recomputed value); **mid-path**, `F=V0/8` (`R=8`); and **near-parity**, `F=V0/1` (`R=1`). A fourth total, **fee-leaning** `F=V0/.5` (`R=.5`), is computed only for three confirmation rows (structure R, each actor rank, low business); for every other profile its zero optimum follows from the critical-ratio identity below and is reported as an identity, not a discovery. These ratios refer to the **scheduled node envelope's valued allowance**, not actual mint or an adopted emissions-to-fees target. They are selected stress controls; 8 is not a transfer of September's year-four rule, and the measured ratio is a one-round observation, not a forecast. Expected pattern, to be confirmed rather than assumed: at the measured and mid-path scales a below-cap actor's optimum sits at its stake cap or at the budget convention; only near parity does a positive interior optimum appear, and then only for actors whose cap lies beyond the stationary point.

For each below-cap, zero-setup profile report the critical ratio `Rcrit=L/[t(1-a)]`, where `R=.94 E P/F`, `a=f/F` and `t` is organizational reward retention. Positive infinitesimal spending pays only for `R>Rcrit`; equality gives zero first-order gain and no positive optimum because of concavity. This locates the regime boundary without adding scenarios. A capped actor or zero reward retention has no finite profitable ratio under the primary fixed-stake rule. Report the corresponding boundary under overrides where applicable; a setup charge requires separate break-even accounting.

The core is **3 structures × 3 market scales × 3 actor ranks × 2 outside-business starts = 54 profiles**, plus the three fee-leaning confirmations. Changing only total fees tests economic scale; D/C and actor overrides test distribution. Do not call 54 profiles 54 observed networks.

## 5. Choices, retained payoff and finite bounds

### 5.1 Standardized core and feasible action

Outside and controlled jobs use a divisible normalized job unit: price `p=1 USDC/job`, execution cost `c=.30 USDC/job`, setup zero. Baseline outside volumes are each pool's fees divided by price. Additional controlled work is real. The core strategic actor assumes full organizational fee/reward retention for a transparent upper-bound comparison; it is not inferred ownership. Honest non-actors keep their source pool's native fee/reward cuts and ownership proxy in every structure so organizational and external-delegator effects remain visible; applying those observations to synthetic roles is an assumption. Do not automatically lower costs when price changes.

For each evaluated state, non-actor service capacity equals its assigned outside jobs and is non-binding by construction. The actor's generic total capacity equals its assigned outside jobs plus the declared additional capacity Qmax; in a niche state, allocate that additional capacity to the niche offer rather than counting it twice. Recompute baseline outside capacity when stipulated demand/routing changes, while preserving the actor's additional Qmax. These capacity premises do not establish observed spare capacity or the feasibility of real expansion.

Each profile has a **one-round** incremental net cash-loss allowance `B=.10 F` and additional capacity `Qmax=.25 F/p` jobs per earning round. `B` is a declared diagnostic convention proportional to network fees, not an estimate of any participant's resources; at the measured scale it is about 82 USDC per round. Where `B` binds, label the stopping point "budget convention" and never present it as a protocol defense; the budget-convention supplement (§6) tests what additional funding changes. B is a net-loss allowance with immediate fee-side recovery, not a gross-invoice funding limit; intra-job advance liquidity and financing are unmodeled. Report gross invoices and recovered fees separately. Under the declared costs and retentions, budget binds before additional capacity; no capacity-limited optimum is claimed. Capacity is still checked for landmarks and diagnostics. Unconstrained opportunity is diagnostic; affordable opportunity must obey these bounds.

For organization reward retention `t`, node-side fee retention `z`, and added gross invoice fees `x=p q_self`:

```
leakage per invoiced USDC L = 1 - .5 z + c/p
cash delta = -L x
value delta = P .94 E t [min((f+x)/(F+x),s)-min(f/F,s)] - L x
```

Use explicit accounting for setup, useful-use value, scores and claims outside this simple core. Outside delegators' receipts are excluded from organizational payoff and separately reported. Net value includes received unsold tokens at stated valuation; it is not realized cash profit.

The stake-cap boundary for an initially below-cap actor is `xcap=(s F-f)/(1-s)`. A capped actor has zero reward headroom. Find the continuous one-period optimum on `[0, min(B/L, p Qmax)]`, including zero, the stake-cap kink and relevant stationary point. On the uncapped segment the candidate is `sqrt(P .94 E t (F-f)/L)-F`. Check all eligible endpoints; never report a sampled-grid maximum as the optimum. Once the cap binds, additional purchases lose cash without adding node reward.

Report the unconstrained one-round profit optimum, feasible one-round optimum, gross reward headroom, critical reward/fee ratio, nonzero break-even spending where it exists, budget/capacity reachability, and marginal versus accumulated return. A zero-return optimum is a required result. Use piecewise roots or bracketed verified roots where a closed form is inconvenient. Closed forms locate action points; every reported payoff is evaluated through EX-06's checked accounting function and independently cross-checked against the algebra.

### 5.2 Progression and impacts on honest participants

For each core profile report at most eight distinct spending points: zero; `.01 F`; `.10 F`; feasible optimum; feasible endpoint; stake-cap boundary; unconstrained optimum; and `x=F` as a harm-only diagnostic. Deduplicate ties. Infeasible points remain explicitly diagnostic and cannot establish affordable conduct. No profitability claim is attached to spending beyond the player's optimum merely because it harms others.

For every point compute all 100 pools. Give each honest pool its baseline and changed node reward, absolute token loss and percentage loss, unchanged outside-fee receipts, net-income effect, fee/stake-limited status and any crossed threshold. Aggregate honest-pool losses and outside delegator effects without hiding individual concentrations. A pool with zero initial reward has an undefined relative loss, not zero or infinity.

At fixed scores, a fee-limited victim's reward ratio is `F/(F+x)`. A stake-limited victim is unchanged until its diluted fee share falls below its stake share. For each victim with positive baseline reward weight `w_j=min(g_j/F,s_j)`, a target fractional loss `d` requires `x=g_j/[w_j(1-d)]-F`. Report the 5%, 10% and 20% landmarks and whether each is affordable, capacity-feasible, before the actor's optimum and before/after its cap. Feasibility uses inclusive endpoints (`<=`), Decimal precision and the verifier's documented numerical tolerance. All fee-limited victims share `x=F d/(1-d)`; in core profiles the 20% landmark equals `p Qmax` and must be labelled "at the capacity boundary", even though it exceeds the core cash-loss allowance. Responses change F while keeping additional capacity fixed, so their landmarks must be recomputed without assuming that equality. These landmarks are illustrative reporting thresholds, not tolerable-harm policy. Also compute collective honest-reward loss landmarks through monotone piecewise accounting; individual percentages are not the aggregate percentage.

For an initially below-cap actor at fixed stake and full scores, the largest dilution that profit-seeking spending can impose on fee-limited victims is reached at the actor's cap: `dcap=(s-a)/(1-a)`, which is the actor's own reward headroom. Under the budget convention the reach is `dB=(B/L)/(F+B/L)`. Report `dcap`, `dB` and `min(dcap,dB)` for every profile beside the landmarks. A landmark beyond that minimum is reachable only through harm-only spending or additional funding and must be labelled so. This is an identity and a required narrative result: a single fixed-stake actor's harm capacity is bounded by its headroom, so the three actor ranks differ in reach because their stake shares differ, not because of rank itself.

Record total fees, node claimability/actual mint, never-minted MFS allowance, pending claims, validator/treasury mint, buyback, burn and net supply. An actor's reward increase need not equal others' loss: previously unavailable allowance can become claimable. Report, at each stopping point, the share of the actor's gain that is previously unminted allowance versus other operators' lost reward; at the measured round roughly four fifths of the node allowance is unminted, so added issuance to all token holders may be the larger effect.

## 7. Evidence interpretation and conclusions

M2.2 maps the tested economic choices onto existing EX-07 claim distinctions and reports what public payment/execution observations and useful-case disclosure would establish on those existing premises. The reused results apply only to EX-07's packeted comparisons and stipulated accurate disclosure of control/funding at n+1. Undisclosed common control, generic full-price work at these volumes and other unpacketed claims are marked "not examined", with their E2/control-evidence dependency, and inherit no EX-07 verdict. No new evidence source, source arrival, false-cut rate or score-to-median response is assumed. A hypothetical score zero may appear only as an algebraic boundary with useful-work cost, never as an observed defense. The controlled invoice price comparison maps onto EX-07's existing payment and price-related claims only: record whether an above-market controlled price would appear in the public records EX-07 already enumerated, inheriting no verdict and adding no detection claim. Creating new packets would require a separate tolerance-first evidence brief; it is outside this defined workload.

Fixed stake is a rule of this study. For each capped position (high-business starts, the capped measured reference and the aligned controls), report as arithmetic only the added stake `dS=(a S - S_i)/(1-a)` (network stake `S`, actor stake `S_i`, fee share `a`) at which the actor's cap would clear its current fee share. Its cost, availability, delay and delegator response are not modeled; they are a named dependency on Slice 5 and P1 (HQ-01), handed to M3 as unresolved.

M2.3 must distinguish arithmetic identities, conditional model results, scenario-ground-truth premises and empirical unknowns. For each consequential conclusion state which networks support it, which weaken/reject it, who benefits and pays, which defense works, and which practical dependency could change it. Preserve original findings under their source-scoped conditions; explicitly qualify any broader claim that additional conditions contradict.

The intended result is a listening-friendly account for each stake position: starting opportunity, affordable one-round profitable progression and stopping point, honest-party losses, bounded response and fixed-stock pacing. Organize it as three principal stories, largest, middle and lower stake, each answering the same questions in order: what opportunity exists, what the actor spends, what it retains, who loses and how much, why it stops, and which changed condition reverses the result. Present the market scales as a path from today's measured condition to a fee-dominant network and state where on that path each defense begins to work. The full matrix supports those stories; it is not the story. Explain which limits bind and which are only checked, name the budget convention as a convention wherever it is the binding limit, and do not present the fixed timelines as multi-round optimal strategies. Classify remaining matters as specification, evidence, incentive analysis or policy. M3 receives unresolved concerns and retained strengths, not a chosen expansion. Full M2 completion, observed dishonesty and owner acceptance do not follow from completing this study.
